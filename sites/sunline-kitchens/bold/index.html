Meet Dana & Priya

Two designers. One promise.

We started Sunline in 2016 after a decade of designing kitchens for volume builders — beautiful on the display floor, compromised by the time they reached real homes. We wanted to do it the other way around: start with how you actually live, then design the kitchen to match.

It's still just us: Dana leads design, Priya runs the projects, and Marco — with us since year one — installs every kitchen. We only take on a handful of projects at a time, which is exactly why we can put a fixed price on every one and stand behind it for seven years.

Straight answers

Questions we get every week

How long will I be without a kitchen?

A Refresh typically runs 2–3 weeks, a Full Reno 4–6 and a Luxe 6–8. We set up a temporary bench with your sink, kettle and microwave on day one, and we confirm the completion date in writing before we start.

Is the fixed price really fixed?

Yes. After the free site measure your quote is locked. The only way the number moves is if you change the scope — and any change is priced and signed off by you before we order a thing.

Is the $190 design fee really credited back?

In full, off your first invoice, when you book any package. If you take the concept elsewhere, the $190 simply covers Dana or Priya's time — no hard feelings, the drawings are yours.

Who actually does the work?

Our own installer Marco runs every site, with the same licenced plumber and electrician we've used for years. We're QBCC licenced (Lic. 15093488), fully insured, and you deal with one number the whole way: ours.

How do payments work?

A deposit secures your build slot, a progress payment falls on cabinetry delivery, and the balance is only due after your handover walkthrough — when you've opened every drawer and you're happy.

Do you do benchtop-only or door-only jobs?

Sometimes. If your cabinetry carcasses are sound, the Refresh package is usually the smarter spend — but book a consult and we'll tell you honestly if a smaller job is all you need. Book online and ask.
